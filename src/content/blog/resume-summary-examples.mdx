Recruiters spend an average of just a few seconds on an initial resume scan. Your summary section — the first thing they read — determines whether they keep going.

## What a Resume Summary Should Do

A strong summary answers three questions immediately: who are you professionally, what's your area of expertise, and what value do you bring? It is not an objective statement about what you're looking for.

## The Formula

```
[Job Title/Identity] with [X years] of experience in [specialty area].
Proven track record of [key achievement type]. Skilled in [2-3 core competencies].
```

## Examples by Career Level

**Mid-Level Professional**
> "Results-driven Operations Manager with 7 years of experience streamlining supply chain processes for mid-size manufacturers. Reduced fulfillment costs by 15% through vendor renegotiation and process automation. Skilled in inventory forecasting, cross-functional leadership, and ERP systems."

**Executive**
> "Strategic COO with 15+ years scaling operations for high-growth SaaS companies from Series B through IPO. Led organizational restructuring that improved operating margin by 8 points. Expert in P&L management, M&A integration, and building high-performing leadership teams."

**Career Changer**
> "Former high school educator transitioning into corporate training and development, bringing 6 years of curriculum design and stakeholder communication experience. Skilled in instructional design, presentation delivery, and performance measurement."

**Entry-Level**
> "Marketing graduate with hands-on experience through two internships in social media strategy and content creation. Proficient in Canva, Google Analytics, and campaign reporting. Eager to bring a data-driven, creative approach to a growth marketing team."

## Mistakes to Avoid

- Writing in third person or using "I" — keep it in a professional, implied-first-person tone
- Vague adjectives with no evidence ("hardworking," "team player") unsupported by specifics
- Repeating your job title verbatim without adding a specialty or differentiator
- Making it too long — 3–4 lines is the sweet spot
