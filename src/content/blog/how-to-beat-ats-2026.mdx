If you've applied to dozens of jobs without a single callback, the problem may not be your qualifications — it may be that your resume never reached a human being. An estimated majority of mid-size and large companies use an Applicant Tracking System (ATS) to filter resumes before recruiters review them.

## What Is an ATS, Really?

An ATS is software that parses your resume into structured data — name, work history, skills, education — and ranks or filters candidates based on keyword matches and formatting compatibility. If the system can't parse your resume correctly, a qualified candidate can be filtered out before a recruiter ever opens the file.

## The Formatting Mistakes That Break ATS Parsing

- **Tables and text boxes.** Many ATS platforms cannot read content inside tables or text boxes, which can cause entire sections to disappear.
- **Headers and footers.** Contact information placed in a header or footer is frequently skipped entirely.
- **Graphics and icons replacing text.** Skill ratings shown as graphics (dots, bars) don't parse as text.
- **Non-standard section headings.** "Where I've Been" instead of "Experience" can confuse parsing logic.
- **Unusual fonts and symbols.** Stick to standard fonts and avoid decorative bullet characters.

## Keyword Strategy That Actually Works

Keyword stuffing hurts more than it helps. Instead:

1. Pull 3–5 real job postings for your target role.
2. Identify recurring hard skills, tools, and certifications.
3. Mirror the exact phrasing used in the posting (e.g., "project management" vs. "managing projects").
4. Integrate keywords naturally into achievement statements, not as a hidden list.

## Structure for Both ATS and Humans

A resume that passes the ATS but reads poorly to a human recruiter still fails. The best approach uses a clean, single-column layout, standard section headings, and achievement-driven bullet points — formatting that works for software and people simultaneously.
