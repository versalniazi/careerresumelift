Most candidates leave money on the table simply because they don't ask. Research consistently shows that negotiating is expected by employers — and rarely results in a rescinded offer when handled professionally.

## Before You Negotiate: Do the Research

Use resources like Bureau of Labor Statistics data, Levels.fyi, Glassdoor, and industry salary surveys to understand the realistic range for your role, experience level, and location before you respond to an offer.

## Script 1: Asking for Time to Consider

> "Thank you so much for the offer — I'm genuinely excited about this opportunity. Could I have until [specific date] to review the details before I give you my decision?"

Never accept or negotiate on the spot. Always take at least 24–48 hours.

## Script 2: Countering the Base Salary

> "I'm really excited about this role and the team. Based on my research for similar positions in [location] and my experience with [specific relevant skill], I was hoping we could discuss a base salary closer to $[target number]. Is there flexibility there?"

## Script 3: Negotiating When There's Limited Budget Flexibility

> "I understand the base salary may be fixed at this level. Would there be flexibility around a signing bonus, additional PTO, or an accelerated performance review timeline?"

## Script 4: Handling a Competing Offer

> "I want to be transparent — I have another offer at $[number]. This role is my first choice, and I'd love to find a way to make the numbers work. Is there room to move closer to that figure?"

## What Not to Do

- Don't give a number before the employer does, if you can avoid it.
- Don't negotiate over email if a call is offered — tone matters.
- Don't frame it as an ultimatum unless you're prepared to walk away.
- Don't forget non-salary levers: signing bonus, equity, PTO, remote flexibility, title, start date.

## After You Reach an Agreement

Always request the final offer in writing before resigning from your current role or declining other opportunities.
